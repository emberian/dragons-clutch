# CFTC Innovation Task Force meeting request draft

Do not submit until counsel has reviewed the factual packet and all placeholders
are complete. The current CFTC form accepts a purpose statement of up to 4,000
characters and attachments up to 10 MB each.

## Proposed form text

**Name:** [legal name]  
**Phone:** [telephone]  
**Email:** [email]  
**Organization:** [individual / proposed entity; do not invent one]

**Purpose of meeting:**

> I request a pre-deployment discussion concerning Dragon's Clutch, a proposed
> open-source Solana protocol for fully collateralized conditional assets over
> objective crypto-native facts. Nothing has been deployed: no program, market,
> transaction client, users, or funds exist. I am seeking guidance before deciding
> whether to implement or personally deploy it.
>
> A user would deposit an immutable Realm's collateral into a market-local
> program vault and receive a complete set of transferable claims over an
> exhaustive, disjoint state partition—for example, terminal token-price bands.
> A complete set may be recombined before resolution. A frozen onchain source and
> deterministic evaluator identify the realized state; the corresponding claim
> redeems. The program permits no debt, margin, liquidation, discretionary
> resolver, or administrative change to live terms. A proposed native
> multilateral batch auction would accept only fully reserved orders and verify
> clearing onchain. Observation, repair, clearing, finalization, redemption, and
> cleanup would be permissionless paid instructions. The intended client is a
> reproducible static GitHub Pages/IPFS application with no custody, proprietary
> router, or required operator backend.
>
> I recognize that event- and price-contingent contracts may be swaps, futures,
> or commodity options and that full collateralization, open source, self-custody,
> and decentralization do not themselves resolve CFTC classification. I would
> like staff's preliminary direction on (1) product classification; (2) whether
> the program and auction implicate DCM, SEF, DCO, FCM, IB, or other registration;
> (3) who would be treated as the operator after immutable deployment; and (4)
> whether a narrow no-action, exemptive, innovation-pilot, registrant-partnership,
> or registration path could permit a small developer to deploy a bounded version
> to Solana mainnet.
>
> Initial subject matter would exclude political, sports, entertainment, and
> subjective events and would use exact crypto-price/path or Solana-state facts.
> Collateral and fee policy are not frozen. I can provide the attached factual
> architecture and authority matrix and would welcome participation from DMO,
> DCR, MPD, and any other staff the Task Force considers appropriate.

## Suggested initial attachments

1. `CFTC_PREMEETING_PACKET.pdf`, rendered from the factual packet.
2. `AUTHORITY_MATRIX.pdf`, rendered from the primary-source matrix.
3. A one-page state-and-funds flow diagram, after counsel approves the labels.

Do not initially attach the entire engineering repository. Keep the first meeting
about classification, persons, and viable procedural paths. Offer the detailed
protocol, proofs, source threat model, and fee alternatives after staff indicates
which facts matter.

## Requested participants

- Innovation Task Force;
- Division of Market Oversight;
- Division of Clearing and Risk;
- Market Participants Division; and
- other Commission staff the Task Force considers appropriate.

## Meeting objectives

An effective first meeting should end with answers or follow-up ownership for:

1. the most likely product and facility classifications;
2. the persons and conduct staff considers legally operative;
3. whether public retail use is categorically unavailable without DCM/DCO
   registration;
4. whether a bounded pilot is worth developing into a formal request;
5. the correct relief type and division(s), if any;
6. the minimum conditions and exhibits for the next submission; and
7. whether staff recommends retaining registered-entity partners before further
   protocol work.

## What not to say

- Do not say the protocol is unregulated, approved, formally verified,
  trustless, or legally operatorless.
- Do not imply an Innovation Task Force conversation is relief.
- Do not describe hypothetical controls, audits, immutability, sources,
  collateral, fees, or entities as completed facts.
- Do not use “spot,” “prediction market,” “option,” “swap,” “clearinghouse,” or
  “exchange” as a legal conclusion; describe mechanics and ask for classification.
- Do not omit material founder control, compensation, holdings, affiliated
  trading, client publication, or market-creation conduct when those facts become
  known.

## 5. Unexplained authority — **instrumented, 3 findings, 1 open**

Checked rather than assumed, because a privilege-shaped instrument does exist
and it would be easy to mistake for this one.

`tools/seam-audit` runs a live `PRIVILEGE` class with a triaged baseline: **70
findings** — `PRIVILEGE_PIN_UNEXEMPTED` 41 (38 `hazard-privilege-pin`) and
`TRANSACTION_LEVEL_SIGNER_CENSUS` 29 (24 `hazard-signer-census`), with
`benign-declared-privilege-census` 3, `checked-caller-excludes-payer` 2,
`benign-payer-pinned-signer` 1, `benign-named-coordinates-only` 1 and
`confirmed-defect` 1. It is a real gate with negative controls.

**It answers a different question, and in the opposite direction.** Its own
docstring states the subject: *"An exact-privilege census is a constraint on the
whole transaction, not on your instruction"* — privileges merge across a
transaction, the fee payer reads `is_signer == true` in every instruction that
names it, so a blanket `is_signer` refusal or an unexempted `is_writable` pin
makes the frame **unsatisfiable for a legitimate builder**. Its negative control
is `5ca145e8`, which did exactly that to three abort routes so "an expired
founding can never be unwound".

That is an **over-constraint / liveness** reader: it finds routes that refuse
what they should admit. C-16's *unexplained authority* is the **under-constraint
/ soundness** question — which signer, PDA or cached role authorises an act, and
whether any authority is asserted rather than derived from an authenticated
record. The `PRIVILEGE` class never asks whether a signer is the *right* signer.
The two point opposite ways, so its 70 findings do not shrink this category by
one.

**So the reader was built** (`9f9f943c`, `e548df96`): `seam-audit`'s seventh
class, `AUTHORITY`, code `AUTHORITY_CACHE_UNDERIVED`.

**The question it asks.** Its subject is the Registry-owned activation cache,
chosen because a **cached role** is where authority is likeliest to go
unexplained: the authentication happens once, in another program, and everything
downstream reads the result. The protocol's settled shape for reading it is
written down in `dclutch-registry-activation-auth-v1` — privilege frame, cache
ownership, address derived from `[ACTIVATION_PDA_DOMAIN_V1, release_set_id]`,
hostile decode, deployment check. A function that decodes the cache while doing
neither that delegation nor, in its own body, both a derivation and an owner
check, is reading a role out of an account whose provenance it never
established.

**Result: 3 findings, all on-chain.**

| site | verdict |
| --- | --- |
| `custody-sbf/src/lib.rs` `authenticate_realm`, `authenticate_premarket_realm` | **benign, verified** — provenance is established one call earlier and carried in the type. `authenticate_market_admission` resolves the cache once and returns `AuthenticatedMarketAdmissionV1`; both variants carry a `cache_bump`, and the realm functions are reachable only by matching on that value (`lib.rs:301-308`). Authenticate once, prove it in the type. Both arms read end to end. |
| `trading-sbf/src/hot_v3.rs` `selected_role_programs_v3` | **`hazard-cache-provenance-unverified`, open** — reads `frame.activation_cache` and selects Claims, Custody and Resolution role programs from it with no derivation, owner check or delegation. The same token argument is *plausible*; **this lane did not establish it.** Owner: Trading. |

The honest difference between those two rows is that the Custody chain was read
end to end and the Trading one was not. `AGENTS.md`'s rule about absent signals
applies to verdicts as much as to measurements: *not shown to be wrong* is not
*shown to be right*, so the second is recorded as a hazard rather than assumed
benign.

**What a clean result would assert, and what it would not.** Silence means every
on-chain cache read either delegates to the shared authenticator or derives and
owner-checks the account itself. It does **not** mean the authority is
*correct*: a function that derives the right address and then reads the wrong
role, or applies a role to an act it does not cover, reads as derived and is
still wrong. Provenance and correctness are different questions and this reader
answers the first — the same distinction §6 draws when it says *swept clean at
the construction sites, correctness not asserted*, and §8.1 draws when it says
an instrument reports a property and never a verdict.

**Its negative control is synthetic and passes**: `authenticate_market` is
silent at HEAD, and deleting its delegation to
`authenticate_activation_cache_bump_v1` makes the reader name it. A checker that
has never caught a defect is decoration; this one is held to the same bar as the
other six.

**Scope, stated because it bounds the claim.** One authority object (the
activation cache), `programs/` only — operator and codec crates decode the cache
to *build* a transaction, where it is not an authority check at all — and one
level of call resolution within a crate, which is what lets it see the
Registry's own local authenticator. Signer-derived authority, PDA-owner
authority and re-used cross-instruction authentications beyond the cache are
**not** covered. **This category is now instrumented, not finished.**

## 8. What a hostile reviewer should distrust, and why

These are the epistemics, not the findings. They are here because a reviewer
who does not know them will repeat the errors this campaign made.

### 8.1 A dead code has at least three causes, and the census names none of them

One instance of each appeared in this campaign:

| cause | instance | what it turned out to be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the route was never built | `0x5006 CustodyRequired` | genuinely dead; R-3 |
| the guard was deleted with its route | the ten `LiabilityBasisSbfErrorV2` codes | withdrawn with `DCLLBX02`, which had no producer and no chain record, so not one was ever raised |
| **the guard was present and I could not see it** | `0x5644 Phase` | **I convicted it as a missing guard. It was reachable, and S7 landed the real one.** |

**The census reports absence; it never reports cause.** A census whose output is
treated as a verdict produces exactly the third row. Every "dead" finding needs
a second method before it becomes an accusation.

### 8.2 The shape to look for: a present test, hollowed out

The sharpest instance anyone found this campaign is not a missing test.

Fees and collateral as geometry

The price of risk

What should a venue charge for?

Not principal. Not redeeming a correct claim. Not carrying a riskless complete set. Adding complete sets to any position changes nothing that matters — the payoff shifts by a constant, the collateral moves in lockstep, no counterparty is involved — so a principled fee must vanish on that diagonal, and once you also refuse to let traders cheapen a fee by fragmenting one order into many, the fee base is forced to be a seminorm on the quotient space where risk actually lives (docs/FEE_GEOMETRY.md §3; docs/research/RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md §3.1). This page is about the two candidates that survive that forcing — and about the honest twist that the mathematics refuses to pick between them.

The candidate: state-contingent dispersion

Every binary-market fee you have ever paid is some cousin of q·p·(1−p) — charge the most when the market is most uncertain. The corpus generalizes it exactly: G(a,p) = Σi<j pᵢpⱼ|aᵢ − aⱼ| / S² — the expected payoff difference between two states drawn from the market’s own implied distribution. It vanishes on complete sets, ignores relabeling, scales linearly with quantity, and rewards expressing your actual net shape instead of a pile of legs. And it is not merely a nice construction: accept the binary calibration and additivity over payoff layers, and G is derived — provably the unique such extension, twice over. paper proof — RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md Props 11–12; FEE_GEOMETRY.md §3 At the kernel’s ceiling of 16 outcomes it costs at most 120 exact integer pair terms to verify — nested loops and one division, no floating point anywhere (FEE_GEOMETRY.md §3; crates/clutch-kernel/src/lib.rs:31).

On the Friday clutch’s cleared prices, Maya’s tent disperses at G = 0.4086 collateral units per lot — under the theoretical ceiling of R/4 = 0.5 for a shape of range 2, as the envelope theorem requires. (Checked, like every number in the illustration, by the site’s arithmetic script.)

The twist the corpus refused to hide

It would be easy to market G as “charging for risk.” The corpus proved the opposite. G is bounded by a quarter of the model-free range — G(a,p) ≤ R(a)/4, with the bound touched only at maximally uncertain two-point prices — and it vanishes as prices go extreme, while the at-risk capital of the same position stays fixed. It measures variability under the market’s own belief, not model-free risk moved. paper proof — RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md Prop 10 Worse, at boundary prices its kernel grows a hole: risk transfer supported entirely on zero-priced outcomes is literally feeless, however large its range — a laundering channel the fee’s own virtue degenerates into at extreme prices. paper proof — RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md Prop 9 So two candidates survive: G, price-weighted, tails nearly free; or the price-free range norm κ′·R(a), manipulation-free but charging a 99-cent claim’s buyer the same absolute fee as a 50-cent claim’s. The choice is economics, not mathematics — the corpus says so in those words — and meanwhile the fee is forced to zero in every configuration currently true in the tree: no fee shape is frozen, and the only settlement that has ever executed is zero-fee (docs/ECONOMICS.md §6). Collateral is the other half of the geometry

The collateral rule is the same geometry read in the other direction: reserve the sup-norm — the maximum component of your position vector. At degrees zero and one every basis vertex is attained, so this is not a conservative bound; it is exactly the supremum of realizable payoff. No slack, no model, no oracle in the requirement. paper proof — RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md Props 1–2; PROVED-MODEL solvency family, CURRENT_TRUTH.md §3 Two structural consequences follow. Settling one market can never raise the requirement on what remains — under SPAN or VaR margining, a hedge leg expiring routinely raises margin on the residual book; here that is impossible, so “margin call” has no referent (Prop 8). And no model-free calendar or cross-asset netting exists at all: whenever no frozen deterministic linkage relates two markets, the honest joint worst case is exactly the sum of the parts — provably, not lazily. Any venue giving you calendar relief is asserting a correlation model; this one refuses to hold one. paper proof — RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md Props 6, 8

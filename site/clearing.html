Why the algorithms are powerful, part 2

The clearing

Nobody on-chain searches. Anyone off-chain may.

Most venues run the matching engine themselves and ask you to trust it. Here the division of labor is inverted. The chain never searches for a clearing; its job is verify-not-find. Anyone — a person, a bot, a competitor’s bot — may compute a clearing of the frozen book off-chain and submit it as a candidate: a price vector, a complete-set imbalance, and the fills. The candidate is trusted for nothing. Every derived quantity — eligibility, per-outcome conservation, cash closure, the score — is recomputed on-chain and checked for exact equality, and the project’s standing language for the winner is “best valid submitted candidate,” never “optimal” — until a certificate says otherwise, which is the punchline below (docs/research/DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md §1).

The walk

A book does not fit in one transaction, so verification streams. The frozen order pages are walked order by order — tombstones of cancelled orders skipped, every surviving order’s funded reservation re-verified — then slice by slice through the pairing, with the relation’s exact state checkpointed between transactions in a 48,750-byte program account. The checkpoint refuses tampering in three layers: a consumed-fold latch, a validated header, and a binding to the exact frozen order set — substitute any page, feed, or the checkpoint itself wholesale, and the walk refuses to resume (docs/design/TIER2_PORTFOLIO_CLEARING_PLAN_2026-08-20.md; crates/clutch-batch/src/relation_v1_stream.rs). In laboratory bank evidence, a 40-order, three-page, four-outcome book with two portfolio buys and three tombstones was placed through the general arm, frozen, and walked to VERIFIED across roughly twenty transactions — with the on-chain verdict and persisted score byte-equal to the host relation’s. What acceptance checks — and what the paper theorem adds

The executable relation rechecks feasibility, conservation, cash closure, the submitted score, and — under the strict policy — full fills or refusal. It does not check an optimality certificate. A paper theorem argues that, for the frozen policy tuple, any accepted candidate has zero duality gap in the LP relaxation and its witness price vector is an optimal dual. Under that theorem, StrictUnderfill acts as the positive-gap detector; that conclusion remains on the paper-proof plane. paper proof — DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md Thm 5.1; falsifiers named in its §11 And when no admissible candidate satisfying the relation exists — in the analyzed cases, exactly when the LP’s optimal dual face misses the admissible price box — the epoch lapses: it clears nothing rather than publish a false number (DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md §6).

⟨scholar⟩ Theorem 5.1 is scoped to the policy tuple (allocation A, AON refused at admission, whole-lot portfolios, no self-crossing) and is a paper proof over the repo’s integer objects — not machine-checked, with its code-level falsifier named (every_accepted_candidate_has_zero_duality_gap) before any language upgrade is permitted (DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md §5.1, §11).

Clearing the batch publishes basis prices

The same accepted object wears a third hat. At degree ≤ 1, every price vector the venue can publish is exactly a probability distribution’s moment vector over the basis. The on-chain object is that basis-price vector, not a unique underlying density. On the degree-one cardinal grid, the named reconstruction Qknot = Σ(pᵢ/S)·δtᵢ represents it exactly; drawing each knot mass across one gap gives the derived histogram pᵢ/(S·g). paper proof — DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md Thm 7.1 Within the no-arbitrage band the specific published point is chosen by a frozen, deterministic score — a policy choice, and the corpus says so plainly rather than calling it a market revelation (DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md §5.3, §9.3).

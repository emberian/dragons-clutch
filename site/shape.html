Why the algorithms are powerful, part 1

The shape of a claim

Degree zero: every prediction market you already know

A degree-zero market divides its question into exhaustive, disjoint, ordered bins. Exactly one bin wins. This is every prediction market you have ever used, and for elections it is the right shape — the world really does pick one cell. For prices it is a lie with good manners: the world resolves to a number on a continuum, and the bin structure converts your belief about that number into a bet on which side of an arbitrary boundary it lands. Hold the “$140–160” bin and a resolution at $159.90 pays you in full; at $160.10 you get nothing. Your P&L cliff-edges at a line somebody drew when they created the market.

Climb the ladder

Degrees one through three replace the bins with open-clamped B-spline Eggs: overlapping claims with local support, never negative, summing exactly to one at every admissible value. That last property — partition of unity — is not a design intention; it is a machine-checked theorem about the emitted basis. That changes the manipulation economics too: nudging the feed near a boundary buys the manipulator value proportional to the nudge — not a full unit per straddled holder, which is what the same nudge is worth on any bins venue (docs/research/RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md §4.1).

⟨scholar⟩ H1/H2: every basis weight is nonnegative and bounded by the denominator, and the weights sum exactly to it at every admissible value. Solvency, complete-set par value, and price normalization all descend from these two hypotheses (docs/research/RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md §1.1; lean/DragonsClutch/BSpline.lean).

Buy the density

A prediction market asks you a yes/no question. A perp asks you a direction. Both throw away almost everything you actually believe. If you think SOL ends the week near $160 — probably between $140 and $180, almost certainly not above $220 — a binary market makes you round that belief to one bit, and a perp makes you convert it into a leverage number and a liquidation price you didn’t want.

Dragon’s Clutch asks a better question: what shape is your belief?

The Friday clutch has eight Eggs — eight hat-shaped claims, one per knot from $100 to $240. Each hat pays its full weight if the week’s time-weighted average price lands exactly on its knot, and fades linearly to zero at the neighbors. The hats overlap, none is ever negative, and at every admissible price they sum to exactly one — which is why one of each, a complete set, is worth exactly one collateral unit no matter what happens. That “sums to exactly one” is one of 184 theorems the project maintains at zero sorry. PROVED-MODEL — CURRENT_TRUTH.md §3; lean/DragonsClutch/BSpline.lean

Maya’s belief is a tent: nothing below $120, rising to a peak at $160, gone by $200. She writes it as a vector — (0, 0, 1, 2, 1, 0, 0, 0) — and that vector is her order. One asset, one atomic fill, one price. No legging into three strikes, no leg risk, no margin rule to trust: the collateral her tent requires is its worst-case payout, read directly off the vector, and settling one leg of anything can never spring a margin call on the rest, because there are no legs and there are no margin calls.

Here is the part that should make the academics sit up. On an options venue, the market’s implied probability density is something you reconstruct: fit a smile, differentiate twice, pray. Here the reconstruction is pre-inverted. A hat claim’s payoff is algebraically identical to a butterfly spread, so the cleared price of the $160 Egg already is the second difference of call prices — the market’s probability mass near $160, quoted directly, tradable directly. When the batch clears at prices p, dividing by the knot gap hands you the density histogram. Nobody computes the market’s belief after the fact; the clearing publishes it, exactly, as a side effect of being verified. paper proof — docs/research/DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md §7.3

The truth about the top rungs

Degrees two and three are real twice over. The clamped rational constructions are machine-checked in the same Lean development — exact split distances, local support, solvency and all PROVED-MODEL — CURRENT_TRUTH.md §3 — and they also run: a degree-2 and a degree-3 market are founded from a bank holding no Clutch account at all, then split, resolved from a sealed source window, and redeemed both internally and by a bearer, with every weight derived in exact u128 integer arithmetic and no floating point anywhere on the path. SBF-EXECUTED · bank · UNPROMOTED — CURRENT_TRUTH.md §3

What is genuinely open at degree ≥ 2 is not the claim plane; it is the price plane, and the difference is not bureaucratic caution. Interior quadratic basis functions peak at 3/4 (cubics at 2/3), so there are price vectors that pass the simplex gate yet are no probability measure’s moments, and the explicit arbitrage against one is executable in the admitted order language: split four complete sets, sell four units of the peaked claim at its limit, keep the complement — zero net outlay, at least one collateral unit at every resolution. At degree ≤ 1 the same position can pay exactly zero, so no arbitrage exists — which is precisely why the simplex gate is the no-arbitrage condition on the tradable span at degrees zero and one, and strictly weaker than it above. paper proof — DUAL_IS_THE_MEASURE.md §7.4 The clearing sees a basis only through partition of unity, so it cannot tell the two cases apart, and no gate in front of it currently does either. The exact test is convex-hull membership in a piecewise-polynomial moment body — plausibly checkable in exact integers, and not built.

One more restriction is deliberate and enforced: at degree ≥ 2 only point evidence resolves. A conservative interval that is not a single value refuses rather than picking a representative, because the interval rule for a smooth basis is an open question and guessing it would be a silent choice about somebody’s payout. So the honest ladder is: the top rungs are built, proved, and executed; a market on them resolves only from an exact observation; and the auction in front of them still admits prices on the simplex, which above degree one is the weaker condition.

One honest edge: eight knots is a coarse curve, the grid is frozen at creation, and payoffs are bounded at the edges — no unbounded upside exists here, by construction. What you get for that is a market that can tell you, to the atom, what it believes.

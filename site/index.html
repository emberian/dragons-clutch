What this is

A market that can hold a shape

Dragon’s Clutch is a machine-native language for beliefs about bounded quantities. A market here is not a yes/no question with a referee; it is a frozen basis of claims — from binary bins up through degree-three smooth splines — cleared by an exact integer batch relation, settled under conservation identities that hold to the atom, on a substrate that refuses rather than approximates. This site explains what that means, why the algorithms are powerful, and why it matters more as the traders get more mechanical. It never argues “use this.” It argues “understand this.”

A question with a frozen answer procedure

Every market freezes its entire answer procedure at creation: which data series counts, over what window, under which statistic, with which arithmetic and rounding rule, and what happens when the evidence is bad. No reporter chooses the value. No committee interprets. A resolution transaction either carries uniquely qualifying authenticated evidence or it is rejected — and when the frozen conditions cannot be met, the market follows its frozen failure rule rather than granting anyone discretion (PROJECT.md §6). Refusal over discretion is the design default at every boundary of this system: hostile bytes, fractional lots, ambiguous evidence, and unadmitted data sources are refused with typed errors before anything mutates.

Deposit once, hold everything: the Clutch

To enter a market you deposit its collateral into that market’s Hoard — a vault whose principal pays claimants and nobody else — and receive claims called Eggs. A Clutch — one of every Egg — is worth exactly one collateral unit no matter how the market resolves, because the basis weights sum to one at every admissible value. That makes complete sets money: mint them by depositing, melt them by merging, park cash in them without taking a view.

⟨degen⟩ The complete set is your risk-free exit and your inventory. Hold “everything except my view” and you’re flat — no funding rate, no liquidation price, redeemable at par before or after resolution.
⟨builder⟩ split adds +q to every outcome balance and debits q cash; merge is its exact inverse; both are pure reclassifications inside the pooled Hoard — no token moves (crates/clutch-kernel; accounting table, CURRENT_TRUTH.md §5).
⟨scholar⟩ This is unitality of the payoff operator: Φ(1) = 1 under H2, so span(1) is the risk-free direction and required collateral moves in lockstep along it — Proposition 4 characterizes these as exactly the counterparty-free value-preserving moves (docs/research/RISK_SUMMED_POSITIONS.md §1.1, §1.4).

The one promise

The protocol is not a leveraged exchange. Its central promise is stated in the project brief, and it is stronger and narrower than the promises trading venues usually make:

For every reachable protocol state, the market-local Hoard covers the maximum payout allowed by the market’s immutable terms. (PROJECT.md §1)

No debt, no margin call, no liquidation order, no insurance deficit, no socialized loss — not as a risk policy that a committee tunes, but as geometry: every claim is prepaid at its worst case, so the states in which a venue would need those machines are unreachable. The Friday clutch · stage 1 of 7 · born

One market, followed across every page of this site: a degree-one smooth claim market on the time-weighted average price of SOL/USD over one frozen week. Eight knots from $100 to $240, twenty dollars apart; denominator D = 64; price scale S = 10,000. Eight Eggs — eight hat-shaped claims, each paying in proportion to how close the week’s TWAP lands to its knot.

Maya (a person) thinks SOL ends the week near $160. She does not want a side; she wants that shape, and this market can hold it. Theo (a person) will take the other side. Sigma (a solver bot — deliberately an AI) will do the heavy computing when the clearing comes. Their story advances one lifecycle stage per page.

The Friday clutch is an illustration. No such market exists anywhere. Its arithmetic is real — every number is generated by a checked script against the repository’s own constants (docs/site-plan/friday_clutch_check.py) — but its existence is fiction, and each stage carries the evidence badge of the mechanism it mirrors.

Where you are

What you are reading about is a local research prototype with an unusual honesty apparatus. Throughout this site, load-bearing claims carry a small capsule naming the exact kind of evidence behind them, in the project’s own deliberately nontransitive vocabulary — a machine-checked theorem is never allowed to impersonate runtime evidence, and a green test bench is never allowed to impersonate a deployment. Follow any badge to The Evidence, where the vocabulary is defined and the single scope note lives. The corpus is unusually honest about what does not exist; this site’s charisma comes from that honesty, not despite it.

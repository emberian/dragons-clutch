campaign. They execute the deployed provider programs and their real ABI and
cryptographic verification locally; they do **not** contain a devnet price and
are not production or provider-availability evidence.

The clone helper normalized ProgramData deployment slots to zero. Restoring
only bytes `[4..12)` to little-endian `460336311` (receiver) and `460336290`
(router) reproduced the original complete-body hashes below. The campaign
constructs those same 45-byte loader headers, asserts the complete hashes, and
asserts both ProgramData keys are the Upgradeable Loader's canonical PDA.

The proof fixture was generated by a small host driver against a disposable
validator carrying the captured ELFs. Its material calls, using the pinned
upstream checkout, were:

```text
dummy_guardians_addresses()
Bridge LegacyInstruction::Initialize(ttl=86400, fee=0, 19 guardians)
pyth_solana_receiver::Initialize(Config { wormhole=router,
  valid_data_sources=[DEFAULT_DATA_SOURCE], fee=1, minimum_signatures=5 })
create_accumulator_message([synthetic PriceFeedMessage], ...)
deserialize_accumulator_update_data(...)
InitEncodedVaa -> WriteEncodedVaa -> VerifyEncodedVaaV1
PostUpdate::populate(...)
```

The generator wrote the exact raw instruction data, signed VAA, Config body,
and resulting price-update body only after the real router and receiver calls
succeeded. The upstream helper uses `thread_rng()` to choose 13 signatures
from deterministic guardians, so regenerating the semantic proof is
reproducible but its exact signature subset is not. The committed VAA and its
SHA-256 below are the byte-reproducibility boundary.

The small host driver used for this capture lived in disposable `/private/tmp`
build infrastructure and is not committed. Consequently, the exact VAA bytes
cannot be regenerated from this repository alone; regenerating them requires
recreating the material calls above against the pinned upstream checkout and
will normally select another valid 13-of-19 subset. This is a named fixture
provenance limitation, not a claim of source-level reproducibility.

`guardian-set-0.account.hex` is the lowercase hexadecimal encoding of the
exact 404-byte GuardianSet account observed after the captured router ELF
executed `LegacyInitialize` with `router-initialize.data`. It is reproducible
offline by the ProgramTest fixture from the pinned router ELF and instruction:
little-endian index zero and count 19, nineteen 20-byte keys, exact creation
and expiration timestamps, and the canonical eight-byte zero allocation tail.
The digest below is over the 404 decoded bytes, not the textual hex envelope.

The two `.so` files are the exact ELF tails of those ProgramData accounts.
The campaign reconstructs the complete Upgradeable Loader ProgramData bodies
with their decoded slots and common upgrade authority, and checks complete-body
hashes before starting the bank. It does not use `ProgramTest::add_program` for
either provider program.

`signed.vaa` and `receiver-post-update.data` were generated by the pinned
upstream test utilities. The VAA contains a synthetic feed id of `0x2a` repeated
32 times, price `100000000`, confidence `6357`, exponent `-8`, publish time
`1787431680`, and 13 valid signatures from the upstream deterministic local
guardian set. Guardian secret keys are not stored here; only the resulting
public signed message is. `router-initialize.data` contains the corresponding
19 public guardian addresses. The captured VAA is immutable even though the
upstream helper randomly selected which quorum subset signed it.
